From Small Town to Big Dreams

Childhood Years

1 I grew up in Pangasinan, where I was raised by my lolo, lola, and tita while my parents worked in Manila. 2 I lived with them for several years, and they became the people who guided me through my early childhood. 3 Even though my parents were far away, my grandparents and aunt made sure I never felt alone. 4 Life in Pangasinan was simple, but it was filled with routines that made me feel secure. 5 I spent most of my days playing around our neighborhood and learning the things every kid naturally picks up. 6 My first language was Ilocano, so that was the language I used daily with the people around me. 7 Because of that, I was not used to speaking Tagalog when I was young. 8 Everything changed when my parents brought me to Manila in grade 2. 9 Moving to a new place at that age was exciting, but it was also difficult. 10 One of the biggest challenges I faced was adjusting to Tagalog, which I was not comfortable with at that time. 11 I had to learn new words and expressions to communicate better with my classmates. 12 It took time to get used to hearing and speaking Tagalog every day. 13 I also had to adjust to a different environment because Manila felt very different from Pangasinan. 14 I studied at Sta. Rosa Catholic School in Pasig, where my mother worked as a teacher. 15 Studying in the same school where my mom taught made my transition a little easier. 16 Back in Pangasinan, I loved playing basketball with my friends almost every day. 17 In Manila, I didn’t have many friends yet, so I often played basketball alone at home. 18 As time passed, I eventually made new friends in grade 3 and grade 4. 19 Around that same time, I also became an altar server at our church. 20 I continued serving faithfully until grade 8, when the COVID-19 pandemic suddenly stopped all activities.

Teenage Years

1 My teenage years were not very different from my childhood because I still spent most of my time studying and trying to get better grades. 2 I continued making friends at school and spending time with them whenever I could. 3 We would play together, laugh, and share small moments that made school life enjoyable. 4 But everything changed when the COVID-19 pandemic hit. 5 I could no longer go to my friends’ houses or play outside as I used to. 6 During that time, I mostly stayed at home and played on my computer to pass the time. 7 Even though I couldn’t meet my friends in person, we stayed connected through the internet. 8 We would chat, call, and play online games together almost every day until 1 a.m. 9 We also helped each other with assignments and schoolwork, which made studying easier. 10 During the pandemic, I sometimes felt very low and struggled with my mental health. 11 Whenever I felt that way, my friends were always there to support me. 12 They would cheer me up, listen to my problems, and make sure I did not feel completely alone. 13 Even though the situation was difficult, staying in contact with my friends helped me get through it. 14 When the pandemic finally ended, I had just reached grade 11. 15 I started studying at Centro Escolar Integrated School in Makati, where I took STEM. 16 At first, it felt strange to be far from home and to commute daily using the MRT or UV Express. 17 Despite that, I found the experience fun and exciting. 18 I met many new people and made new friends who shared the same interests. 19 School life became busy with new challenges, but I enjoyed learning new things and growing academically. 20 Overall, my teenage years taught me the importance of friendship, support, and adapting to change.

College Experience

1 When I started college, I felt nervous on the first day because I knew I would meet people who were really good at studying. 2 I was scared that I might be left behind compared to my classmates. 3 In the first semester, I worked hard and tried to do all my assignments ahead of time. 4 I studied regularly and focused on understanding the lessons, which helped me keep up with my subjects. 5 Everything felt manageable, and I was confident in my abilities during that time. 6 But when the second semester began, I faced a major challenge that humbled me quickly. 7 Analysis 1, which is calculus, became very difficult for me. 8 I struggled a lot and almost failed the subject if it were not for the help of my friends. 9 They guided me through problems, explained concepts, and encouraged me to keep going. 10 Thanks to their support, I was able to pass and continue with my first year successfully. 11 My first year of college was full of fun and exciting moments, with both ups and downs. 12 I learned how to manage my time, balance studying with social life, and ask for help when I needed it. 13 Now, I am in my second year and getting closer to graduating, which makes me feel proud and motivated. 14 In the first semester of my second year, I struggled again in Analysis 1 during prelims, getting a 4.0, which is a failing grade. 15 I realized I needed to focus more, so I decided to lock in and work even harder. 16 By midterms, my grade improved to 3.0, showing that my effort was paying off. 17 In the finals, I got a 2.25, which made my parents proud, and they gave me shoes and t-shirts to celebrate my achievement. 18 Now, in the second semester, I am striving to survive and succeed in college while continuing to learn and improve. 19 I have learned the importance of perseverance, hard work, and relying on friends for support. 20 No matter how challenging it gets, I am determined not to stop until I graduate and achieve my goals.
